Lauren is a mixed-mathods (mostly qualitative) researcher focussed on human-centred cybersecurity. Particularly looking at mis/disinformation, GenAI literacy, and digital literacy within vulnerable groups. 

Lauren completed their PhD at Northumbria University in 2025 and their thesis explored how individuals correct misinformation within their own family networks and what technology can do to support misinformation correction between family members (turns out: not much). They have since explored how speculative designs of urban infrastructure to limit face-to-face misinformation spread (Funded by Northumbria Urban Futures: co-PI), AI and media literacy for young people (CO-I; RAI Skills; RAI Collaboration), and media, privacy, security and digital literacies for older adults (RA: British Academy; CO-i: DSIT). 

Lauren's current work is exploring how young people understand harmful encounters in immersive environments, such as games, through a SPRITE+ funded project: Towards AI Supported Moderation to Promote Child Safety Online (PI). This project aims to ensure that future AI moderation tools are built with the childrens' voices at the centre of designs; and to engage with children and key stakeholders to understand the challenges in moderating immersive environments. 

Lauren is also involved in the very exciting RE:PLAY project (Co-I) - exploring how playful learning is/can be used in a higher education setting; and their work on AI, media, digital and privacy literacy for vulnerable groups (under 18s, over 60s) is ongoing.
